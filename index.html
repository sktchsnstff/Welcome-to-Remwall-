Founding & Early History:

Remwall was originally a small settlement in 1692 founded by Remy Walton,
after helping his friends and family escape prosecution during the witch trials
in Massachusetts. Though the population started small, around fifteen people,
the settlement eventually became a great haven for travelers on the run from
the church or politics, with many passing by and eventually taking permanent
residence. By around 1712, the population had more than multiplied
by dozen, with estimates ranging between around 180 and 200 local residents,
with some varying from Massachusetts, whilst others came from Connecticut,
New Hampshire, or New York.
This led to the need for a stronger structure of laws and government to be
established, especially if they wanted to keep their security and trade with
other towns. As such, on September 14 of 1713, Remy's brother: Nicholas
Walton was appointed as the first governor of Remwall, after his brother's
passing on December 21st, 1712.
From there, Remwall continued to grow, even past the French and Indian
War and even the revolution. This was caused by its quick rise to notoriety
amongst American patriots for its usage of colonial spies but also its high
trade and market, despite its obscure nature. Curious by this, many American
tradesmen and even tourist began to visit the town, bringing even more
attention to the town, as profit was used to further establishment.
From this, Remwall developed a rich culture, not just from its local history
and obscurity but also from its diverse population from across America, and
even beyond it, to this day.
To many, Remwall is not just a city; it is alive. Despite its odd history and
small size, Remwall continues to live to this day along with its legacy.

About the Founders:

Remy Walton:
Born on August 13th 1668 in Massachusetts, Remy Walton was a known town
shrieker and controversial messenger; his nomadic lifestyle and tendency to
speak without filter often leading to him being considered a nuisance.
This became further exemplified as he grew older, with his deep interest in
entomology and undiagnosed Tourette's syndrome leading to him being called
a madman and a witch.
This led to his life being threatened, and his family being caught in the
crossfire, forcing the run from his former profession and church. Riding on
horseback and eventually on foot, he led him and his family to a small plot of
isolated woodland in the southeastern part of Massachusetts, which he
decided to claim as his own, after discovering that it seemingly had decent
soil, a near water source, and was seemingly untouched by Native populations.
He would then come to call this new settlement "Nespiti", combining the
Greek words for "new" and "home", "spíti" and "néos".
With the help of his family, friends, and his eventual wife, he began to build
the town into a small but still comforting place to live, with his charming
personality and vigor for expressing one's own "anomolies" attracting many
outcasted people to stay and make residence.
From there, he lived a much more peaceful life; living as a farmer with his
wife, until his eventual passing in 1712 from an unknown infection, most
likely caused by untreated frostbite.
Remy's legacy still lives on through his brother and wife, with a wooden
statue made by his grave to remember him, even in death.

Charles Walton:
Born on January 3rd 1673, Charles Walton completely contrasted his brother
in bounds that even their family could tell at a glance. Raised his entire life
in Salem, Charles saw no reason to leave the safety of his home and explore
the world. To him, he had a purpose for why he was born in this family,
and he was going to uphold this purpose, until the day he passed.
Simarily to Remy, Charles grew up in a dysfunctional household; his mother
becoming infertile after giving birth to him and his twin sister, Fionna, whilst
their father condemned both his mothers, siblings, and Charles with his
wrath in order to teach them the importance of being a "Civilized child of
Christ".
Charles took this abuse as a sign to act as a mediator for his difficult family,
often times taking the blame for his own sibling's faults, as he became a
protector for them even when no one asked.
As he grew, he eventually became a well-known and devoted preacher in
Salem, using his charismatic and persuasive skills to help those which have
been burdened with misfortune or grief. This earned him both the respect
of the town and his father, allowing him to make a stable living for himself
and take care of himself and siblings.
This would eventually come crashing down on them, when the witch trials
began in Salem. Though his position had protected him enough from the
corruption of the people, this would only put more attention on his
brother and sister. And despite being able to protect them for a couple of
months, Remy's infamy and annoyance with the town, in addition to Riley's
affair against her husband would eventually lead to their fleeing of Salem
with Remy, Riley, their mother, and six friends.
From there, he managed to make a somewhat stable living for himself and
his remaining siblings. Partially abandoning his previous life as a preacher,
Charles remained faithful, but chose to pursue in the job of law and
government, helping his brother establish Nespiti through trial and error
until he eventually became the first governor. He then continued to the
expansion of Nespiti, letting the small town grow and renaming it to Remwall
after his brother only 6 months before his passing in August of 1718.
